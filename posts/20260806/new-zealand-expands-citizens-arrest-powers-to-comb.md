In a move aimed at restoring law and order and providing greater protection for businesses, New Zealand's parliament on Thursday (Aug 6) passed the Crimes Act Amendment Bill, significantly expanding citizens' arrest powers. Justice Minister Paul Goldsmith highlighted that the legislation is designed to ensure retailers are effectively protected and empowered to prevent offending, while also ensuring offenders are caught and deterred.

Minister Goldsmith underscored the severe impact of retail crime, noting the economic cost runs into billions of dollars annually, and that retailers and security guards frequently face abuse and assault. The new law is a direct response to these challenges.

Key provisions of the new legislation include:

*   **Expanded Intervention:** Citizens will now have the authority to intervene and stop any offense under the Crimes Act at any time of day. This removes previous restrictions where certain powers, particularly those related to theft of goods valued under NZ$1,000 (approximately US$587), were limited to between 9 PM and 6 AM.
*   **Reasonable Force Clarified:** The law clarifies that reasonable restraints and reasonable force may be employed when making an arrest or defending property, offering clear guidelines for citizens exercising these powers.
*   **Mandatory Police Contact:** Any individual making a citizen's arrest is legally required to contact the police promptly and follow their instructions.
*   **New Offenses Introduced:** The Bill also establishes new offenses, including those for assaulting first responders or frontline corrections workers, introduces three new 'coward punch' offenses to tackle unprovoked assaults, creates a new infringement regime for shoplifting, and strengthens existing laws against human trafficking and people smuggling.

The majority of this comprehensive legislation is anticipated to come into effect within a week, marking a swift implementation of these new measures.
