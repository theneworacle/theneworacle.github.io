The 2026 midterm elections are proving to be a watershed moment for the intersection of artificial intelligence and political campaigning. From slick, seemingly innocuous spots to outright 'deepfake' attacks, AI-generated advertisements are becoming a ubiquitous, yet often invisible, force in shaping public opinion. However, a critical issue has emerged: a profound lack of transparency, with most AI ads failing to disclose their synthetic origins, even in states where such disclosures are legally mandated.

Research indicates a concerning trend: of the hundreds of AI-generated or enhanced political ads identified during the current election cycle, a staggering 69% offered no disclosure of AI tool usage. Even more alarming, in states that *do* have laws requiring AI disclosure in political communications, only 29% of ads complied, a figure paradoxically lower than the 32% compliance observed in states with no such regulations at all.

A prime example surfaced in New York, where a Republican candidate allegedly used AI to generate a negative ad against the Democratic incumbent, depicting them in a conversation that painted them in a poor light. The ad's AI origin went undisclosed, despite New York state law, prompting a formal complaint to the Board of Elections. This incident underscores the challenges in enforcing existing legislation and highlights the ease with which AI can be leveraged for potentially deceptive purposes.

While some AI ads are deployed for humorous or satirical effect, like a Michigan Republican candidate portraying himself as a superhero or a North Carolina Senate candidate using AI to comically contort his opponent's limbs, the widespread absence of disclosure blurs the lines between jest and manipulation. Experts like Scott Babwah Brennen of New York University's Center on Technology Policy emphasize that misinformation is a systemic issue within the media environment, calling for broader, more effective regulation beyond simple labels.

The stakes are high, with AI companies pouring an estimated $265 million into midterm campaigns, indicating a growing financial influence on election outcomes. This substantial investment also fuels public backlash, particularly concerning AI data centers, which have become a bipartisan rallying cry. In response, fact-checking organizations are collaborating to debunk deceptive AI ads, and legal challenges are emerging, such as a police report filed in Texas over deepfake AI ads, highlighting the nascent legal battles over this technology.

As campaigns grapple with the evolving norms and laws surrounding AI, a period of uncertainty prevails. The current landscape poses significant questions about the future of democratic transparency and the potential for AI to influence elections in ways voters may not even perceive. Without robust and enforceable disclosure requirements, the unseen hand of AI could cast a long shadow over the integrity of our electoral processes.
