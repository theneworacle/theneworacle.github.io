Tensions in the South China Sea have once again flared, with the Philippines accusing China of deploying water cannons against its vessels near the disputed Scarborough Shoal for a second consecutive day. The Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) reported that a significant contingent of 12 China Coast Guard vessels and three Chinese maritime militia vessels were involved in the latest incident.

According to the PCG, Philippine government vessels were engaged in routine patrols and delivering fuel subsidies to Filipino fishermen, exercising their rights in traditional fishing grounds within the Philippines’ exclusive economic zone. During these operations, a Chinese vessel allegedly approached within seven meters of a Philippine fisheries boat, creating a serious risk of collision, and subsequently fired water cannons at two Philippine ships. The PCG strongly condemned these actions, labeling them as dangerous and unprofessional, and asserted the Philippines' sovereign rights which 'cannot be lawfully impeded by any foreign power.'

In response, China's Coast Guard stated it had taken 'control measures' against Philippine vessels that it claimed were 'illegally gathering' in the disputed waters. Beijing defended its actions as 'reasonable, lawful, and beyond reproach,' urging the Philippines to 'immediately stop the infringement, provocation and hype.'

These events at Scarborough Shoal follow closely on the heels of another confrontation at the Second Thomas Shoal, approximately 650km away, where a Filipino sailor was reportedly injured after being hit with a baton by Chinese coastguard personnel. Both nations have responded by summoning each other’s ambassadors in protest, underscoring the severity of the diplomatic rift.

The escalating maritime disputes unfolded amidst regional meetings of Southeast Asian foreign ministers in Manila, which saw the participation of Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi and United States Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Secretary Rubio publicly voiced concern over Beijing's actions in the sea, calling them 'disturbing' and reaffirming the United States' unwavering support for the Philippines, a long-standing treaty ally.

The situation in the South China Sea remains a critical point of geopolitical contention, with China asserting sovereignty over vast swathes of the waterway, claims that overlap with the exclusive economic zones of several Southeast Asian nations, including the Philippines.
