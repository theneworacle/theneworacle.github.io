The National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), one of the nation's most esteemed institutions, is grappling with a monumental crisis after its utilization of Artificial Intelligence (AI) to monitor and prevent fraud during recent entrance examinations. This technological intervention, intended to uphold academic integrity, has instead led to the annulment of thousands of prospective student admissions, plunging many into a state of limbo.

At the heart of the scandal is the university's decision to deploy AI-powered proctoring during online admissions tests. While the primary intention was to ensure a fair and honest assessment process, the AI system flagged a significant number of irregularities. This detection led to the invalidation of exams and put an estimated thousands of admissions in doubt, prompting widespread concern among students and their families regarding their university enrollment.

The incident has ignited a crucial discussion surrounding the role of AI in education, particularly in high-stakes environments like university admissions. While the use of AI as a tool to detect and prevent cheating is largely viewed positively for its potential to maintain the credibility of academic qualifications, the UNAM case presents a complex scenario.

According to Raúl Rojas, an AI specialist and mathematician who reviewed the results, the improvement in scores was "not typical." He estimated that as many as half of the 158,700 test takers may have cheated. However, Rojas also noted that it was "not clear that artificial intelligence had played a central role" in the *cheating methods themselves*. Instead, he concluded, the majority of cheaters probably resorted to an older, more traditional method: "the cheat sheet." This suggests that while AI was effective in *detecting* anomalies, the underlying issue of widespread dishonesty may have stemmed from conventional practices, possibly exacerbated by the online exam format.

The large-scale annulment raises critical questions about the accuracy and criteria used by the AI system for flagging, as well as the need for robust human oversight. Concerns about potential algorithmic bias and the fairness of penalizing a large cohort based on AI detection are now central to the debate, even as the AI successfully exposed a deeper problem of academic integrity.

UNAM's president, Leonardo Lomelí, has affirmed the university's commitment to uncovering the truth and supporting students who genuinely prepared for the exams. The ongoing investigation aims to clarify the extent of the alleged cheating, the prevalent methods used, and the role of the external company contracted to administer the exams. This incident serves as a stark reminder for educational institutions worldwide to carefully consider the ethical implications, transparency, and impact on students when integrating AI into their critical processes.

As the academic community grapples with the broader implications of artificial intelligence, the UNAM scandal stands as a poignant example of how AI can serve as a powerful diagnostic tool, exposing existing challenges in academic honesty and prompting critical reflection on its responsible implementation.
