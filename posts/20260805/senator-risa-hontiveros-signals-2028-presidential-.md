Philippine Senator Risa Hontiveros recently announced her openness to a presidential bid in the country's 2028 elections, particularly if she garners support from the opposition. This significant declaration was made during a thought-provoking discussion hosted by the Harvard Yenching Institute, where she also critically evaluated the current Philippine government.

Hontiveros, a prominent opposition figure since her 2016 election to the Senate, used her platform to highlight concerns over government corruption, especially regarding the construction of substandard infrastructure that leaves citizens vulnerable during natural disasters. Citing recent earthquakes near Mindanao, Cebu, Davao Oriental, and Surigao del Sur, she lamented how corruption impedes crucial relief efforts and exposed what she called 'human folly' amidst shifting tectonic plates, both physical and political.

Her potential presidential platform would focus on several key areas. Hontiveros has been a staunch advocate for investigating and combating Philippines Offshore Gaming Operations (POGOs) and cryptocurrency scam hubs, exposing their role in facilitating human trafficking. She detailed disturbing cases where Filipino women and women from other Southeast Asian countries were trafficked, forced into scamming under inhumane conditions, and subjected to sexual harassment. She noted that addressing these crimes is intertwined with the Philippines' 'complicated relationship with China,' particularly instances of Chinese nationals allegedly posing as Filipino officials to protect POGOs.

Domestically, Hontiveros continues her years-long battle to legalize divorce in the Philippines, one of only two countries globally (alongside the Vatican) where it remains illegal. Despite facing political hurdles in a predominantly Catholic nation, she persists, proposing a 'dissolution of marriage bill' as an expansion of grounds for legal separation, offering those who 'want and deserve a second chance at life and love and family.'

On foreign policy, Hontiveros called for a broader coalition of like-minded nations to address China's significant presence in the Philippines, including its national energy grid. She criticized former President Rodrigo Duterte's administration for its tilt toward China, describing those years as a 'nightmare' for many Filipinos. She expressed common ground with the current Marcos administration's pivot back towards the West, emphasizing the importance of preventing 'hot hostilities' in the West Philippine Sea and expressing solidarity with Taiwan.

She concluded her address by advocating for the creation of safe spaces for young people, urging an end to government monitoring of political views and the practice of 'red-tagging' – labeling left-wing activists as terrorists. Hontiveros stressed the historical role of youth activists in counterinsurgency and the 'War on Drugs,' highlighting their importance as 'our hope for the future.'

Early signs of grassroots momentum are already visible, with 'Risa Na' movements launching in Bacolod and Cebu to build support for her potential campaign. While social sentiment appears largely positive with some debate, current polls for the 2028 presidential race indicate Vice President Sara Duterte and Naga City Mayor Leni Robredo are presently leading, setting the stage for a potentially dynamic and competitive political landscape.
