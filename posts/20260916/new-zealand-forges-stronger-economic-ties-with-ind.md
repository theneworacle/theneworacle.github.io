In a decisive move that signals a new era of economic partnership, the New Zealand Parliament has officially passed legislation to enact a free trade agreement (FTA) with India. The bill received robust bipartisan support, passing with a commanding vote of 93-29, underscoring the nation's commitment to strengthening its trade relations with one of the world's fastest-growing economies.

This landmark agreement is set to bring "immediate and substantial" benefits to New Zealand exporters, as highlighted by Trade Minister Todd McClay. Under the terms of the deal, tariffs on approximately 95% of New Zealand's exports to India will either be eliminated entirely or significantly reduced. More than half of all products covered by the agreement are slated to become duty-free from the moment the deal officially takes effect, which is anticipated to be later this year.

Conversely, the agreement will also grant duty-free access for all Indian goods entering the New Zealand market, fostering a more balanced and mutually beneficial trade environment. Beyond goods, New Zealand has pledged a significant investment of US$20 billion in India over the next 15 years, further solidifying the long-term strategic partnership.

The trade agreement, originally signed in April, is a culmination of extensive negotiations aimed at boosting two-way trade. In the year leading up to June 2026, trade between New Zealand and India reached NZ$3.99 billion, with India establishing itself as New Zealand's ninth-largest export market for goods and services. The full implementation of the deal awaits the completion of respective ratification procedures by both nations.

Public sentiment regarding the New Zealand-India trade deal has been largely positive, reflecting an optimistic outlook on the economic opportunities and deeper bilateral ties that this agreement is expected to foster.
