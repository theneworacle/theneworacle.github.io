The academic world is grappling with a new ethical frontier, and Dartmouth College has found itself at the epicenter. Santiago Schnell, the institution's provost and a prominent figure, has resigned from his position amidst an "AI uproar," a controversy stemming from his undisclosed use of artificial intelligence in his academic and journalistic work.

An independent faculty committee at Dartmouth launched an investigation after media outlets, including the student newspaper *The Dartmouth*, published analyses revealing that a significant portion of Schnell's recent written output, including academic articles and op-eds, was generated by AI tools without proper disclosure. The committee's findings were damning: Schnell not only used AI tools like Claude and ChatGPT to generate content, revise figures, and even create peer review reports, but he was also dishonest about this usage. These actions, the committee concluded, demonstrably fell short of Dartmouth's standards for academic integrity and honesty, violating its Code of Ethical Conduct and Academic Honor Principle.

This incident casts a stark light on the complexities universities face as AI technology rapidly integrates into academic life. Dartmouth itself holds a unique place in AI history, credited with coining the term at a 1956 conference, and its current president, Sian Leah Beilock, has publicly advocated for embracing AI. However, this public embrace has seemingly come without sufficiently clear institutional policies, leaving faculty and students navigating murky waters. As creative writing professor Jeff Sharlet aptly put it, Schnell's situation provides a clear example of "what not to do," reinforcing the fundamental importance of academic integrity.

To write an academic paper on chemical kinetics, Schnell asked Claude, Anthropic's generative AI chatbot, to "imagine that you are a reviewer for this paper with expertise," according to logs reviewed by the committee. He then asked it to configure new figures, make revisions, and generate a referee report, which is typically used to peer review a piece. He also told Claude that he asked ChatGPT for help as well, prompting it to write an introduction to his paper. He then used Claude to review ChatGPT's responses. The paper, which was published in September in Chemistry Europe, an association of European chemists, did not include any disclosures of his AI use. The committee also found that Schnell asked Claude to review a forthcoming publication on academic freedom and religion in university governance, and prompted the chatbot to assess if the AI tool in his work gave any "signatures of AI writing."

The departure of Schnell, with Matthew Delmont now serving as interim provost, underscores the urgent need for transparent and explicit guidelines on AI use in published work across all academic disciplines. While social sentiment surrounding the resignation has been largely positive, it also reflects an ongoing, vital debate about the broader implications of AI in academia and the paramount importance of disclosure. As AI continues to evolve, universities must proactively establish robust ethical frameworks to ensure that innovation does not come at the cost of integrity.
