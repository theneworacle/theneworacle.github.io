New Zealand has recently made a significant declaration, identifying China as the most persistent and capable state-backed cyber threat it faces. This assertion from Wellington has brought cybersecurity concerns to the forefront of its international relations.

In a swift and direct response, Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun urged New Zealand to cease what China describes as "spreading false information about cyber security related to China." Guo emphasized that China "opposes and resolutely fights hacker attacks in accordance with law, and opposes the dissemination of cyber security disinformation for political purposes." Beijing's official stance calls for New Zealand to "stop spreading lies and stoking discord," suggesting instead that both nations should work towards fostering mutual understanding and trust.

This exchange highlights a growing tension in the digital realm between New Zealand and China, reflecting broader global anxieties over state-sponsored cyber activities. While New Zealand is focused on national security and protecting its digital infrastructure, China views these accusations as politically motivated disinformation campaigns. The diplomatic friction underscores the complex challenges of navigating international relations in an increasingly digital world, where cybersecurity concerns are becoming central to national defense and foreign policy.
