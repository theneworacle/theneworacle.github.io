The rugby world is abuzz as New Zealand's All Blacks have taken their concerns directly to World Rugby regarding the scrum tactics employed by their formidable rivals, the South African Springboks. This move comes hot on the heels of the All Blacks' commanding 33-16 victory over the Springboks in their first test match, a win that saw New Zealand ascend to the top of the world rankings.

All Blacks coach Dave Rennie articulated his team's grievances, suggesting that the Springboks were not consistently scrummaging 'squarely' during the previous encounter. Rennie expressed his hope that by bringing these issues to World Rugby's attention, the officiating, particularly by referee Angus Gardner in the upcoming second test, would ensure a more equitable contest in the set-piece.

On the other side of the scrum, the Springboks have reportedly 'hit back at accusations' regarding their tactics. Key figures such as Rassie Erasmus and Jaco Peyper have asserted that they have received no negative feedback from World Rugby concerning their scrummaging. Despite their significant defeat in the first test, the Springboks are widely expected to deliver a "massive improvement" in the second match. History shows that South Africa rarely loses two tests in a row, especially at home, making a fierce backlash highly anticipated.

Social media sentiment surrounding this rugby rivalry remains largely positive, albeit with a healthy dose of debate reflecting the spirited exchanges both on and off the field. As both teams prepare for what promises to be an intense second test, the focus on scrum integrity adds another intriguing layer to one of rugby's greatest rivalries.
