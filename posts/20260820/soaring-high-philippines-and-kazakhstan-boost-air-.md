    title: "Philippines, Kazakhstan Expand Air Service Rights With Turkey"

In a significant move set to bolster international travel and trade, both the Philippines and Kazakhstan have independently expanded their air service agreements with Turkey. These bilateral arrangements are poised to increase passenger capacity and open up a wider array of travel opportunities.

For the **Philippines**, the new agreement with Turkey doubles the permitted flight frequency between Manila Ninoy Aquino International Airport (MNL) and Istanbul Airport (IST) from 7 to 14 times weekly. Currently, Turkish Airlines operates this route daily using Boeing 777-300ER aircraft. A notable aspect of this agreement is the removal of frequency limitations for services involving secondary Philippine airports, including Clark International Airport, Mactan-Cebu International Airport, and Davao International Airport. This critical change could pave the way for new direct routes to and from these regional hubs, bypassing Manila and simplifying international travel for many.

In 2025, approximately 110,400 two-way passengers traveled between Turkey and the Philippines, with Manila handling nearly 93% of this traffic. The enhanced agreement is expected to stimulate further growth in this market.

Meanwhile, **Kazakhstan's** agreement with Turkey is even more expansive, nearly doubling the number of permitted weekly passenger flights between the two countries from 120 to a substantial 224. This will significantly increase capacity on core routes connecting Istanbul and Ankara with major Kazakh cities like Astana, Almaty, and Shymkent. Additionally, leisure services from popular Turkish destinations like Antalya and Bodrum to Kazakh cities will see a considerable rise.

Unlike the Philippine-Turkey route, the Turkey-Kazakhstan market is already highly competitive, with Air Astana, Turkish Airlines, and Pegasus Airlines as leading operators. In 2025, about 1.07 million two-way passengers traveled between Turkey and Kazakhstan, highlighting the robust demand in this corridor.

Beyond passenger flights, the Turkey-Kazakhstan agreement also allows for up to 38 scheduled cargo frequencies per week on a reciprocal basis. Both nations have also committed to deepening cooperation in crucial aviation areas such as safety, security, training, maintenance, ground handling, airport operations, and digitalization. This holistic approach signifies a long-term commitment to enhancing the entire aviation ecosystem.

Overall, these expanded air service rights are a positive development, promising increased connectivity, economic benefits through tourism and trade, and strengthened diplomatic ties between the involved nations. The general sentiment surrounding these agreements is largely positive, reflecting optimism for the future of air travel in these regions.
