Will AI lead to a jobless future? Not according to top economist Steve Hanke, who offers a refreshing counter-narrative to the prevailing anxieties surrounding artificial intelligence and its potential to displace human workers. Hanke asserts that "simple economics" will act as a natural barrier, preventing companies from simply firing their entire workforce and replacing them with AI.

Hanke's skepticism extends beyond just job displacement. He has publicly labeled AI as "wildly overhyped and potentially dangerous." This perspective puts him at odds with some prominent tech figures, notably Elon Musk, especially concerning the proposed solution of a universal basic income (UBI) to mitigate AI-related job losses. Hanke argues that a UBI is not guaranteed to prevent inflation, challenging the efficacy of such a measure.

While Hanke's views present a departure from much of the public discourse, social sentiment regarding his arguments on AI and job displacement is described as "mostly positive, with some debate." This indicates that while there's a degree of agreement with his economic rationale, the conversation remains dynamic with differing opinions on the complex interplay between AI and the future of work. His insights provide a valuable economic lens through which to examine the ongoing AI revolution.
