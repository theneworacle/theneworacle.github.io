The global energy landscape is undergoing a profound transformation, with the spotlight increasingly turning to natural hydrogen – a clean, carbon-free energy source produced naturally underground. Estimates by the U.S. Energy Department suggest trillions of metric tons could be hidden beneath the Earth's surface, sparking a worldwide quest for viable deposits. And now, the hunt has shifted significantly towards the Philippines.

Energy startup Koloma, backed by major investors like Bill Gates' Breakthrough Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, and Amazon, has been at the forefront of this global exploration. After extensive surveying across the U.S., Australia, and other undisclosed locations, Koloma has set its sights on the Philippines, specifically gaining access to develop potential hydrogen well sites in Zambales Province, west of Manila.

According to Koloma CEO Pete Johnson, the Philippines boasts "the largest natural hydrogen seeps anywhere on the planet," leading to strong indications of "very large accumulations" beneath the surface. This geologically active region, famously home to Mount Pinatubo, presents ideal conditions for natural hydrogen formation.

The implications for the Philippines are substantial. As one of Asia's fastest-growing economies, the nation currently relies heavily on imported energy, particularly diesel and liquefied natural gas (LNG), to power its rapid modernization. The discovery and commercial extraction of natural hydrogen could provide a domestic, cost-competitive alternative, significantly bolstering the country's energy security. Johnson even envisions the Philippines evolving into an "energy or energy product exporter–potentially ammonia or something like that."

Scientific discoveries corroborate this potential. Researchers have reported "exceptionally high" outgassing of natural hydrogen from a site in the Philippines, with more than 800 tonnes seeping annually. This suggests that millions of tonnes could be trapped underground, far exceeding quantities found in other notable sites globally.

In response to this immense potential, the Philippine government has already taken proactive steps. President Marcos confirmed that multiple hydrogen power projects have been initiated, and the country has opened auctions for exploration rights across several zones where natural hydrogen 'gas seeps' have been identified.

While the prospect is exciting, Koloma emphasizes a methodical approach. Confirming the commercial viability and extractable quantities will take at least two years. Johnson underscores the importance of rigorous exploration to build a sustainable industry, stating, "We've found large accumulations. We're not sitting on something that's big and commercial yet."

Despite the nascent stage of commercial production, the buzz around natural hydrogen in the Philippines is palpable. Social sentiment surrounding these developments is largely positive, reflecting the nation's hope for a cleaner, more secure energy future. As the global energy transition accelerates, the Philippines could soon emerge as a pivotal player, tapping into Earth's natural reserves to power its growth and contribute to a more sustainable world.
