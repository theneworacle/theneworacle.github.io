New Zealand is embarking on a significant defence modernisation initiative, with plans to purchase long-range one-way strike drones. This strategic move, announced by Defence Minister Chris Penk, is aimed at bolstering the nation's defence capability in what is described as an "increasingly unpredictable" international security environment.

The government has allocated up to NZ$300 million (approximately A$249 million) within its Defence Capability Plan for this acquisition. These 'one-way strike drones' are defined as human-operated systems designed to deliver an explosive to a target without returning to their launch point. They represent a lower-cost alternative to traditional missiles, offering a pragmatic approach to enhancing military readiness.

A key aspect of this procurement is a strong preference for local innovation and industrial capability. New Zealand businesses are actively encouraged to respond to requests for information, leveraging the country's expertise in advanced manufacturing, digital technologies, and uncrewed systems. This focus on local suppliers is expected to foster domestic skills and strengthen the national defence industry.

Beyond strike capabilities, the government also plans to initiate research into unarmed long-range uncrewed aircraft for maritime surveillance, further expanding New Zealand's defence and security infrastructure.

This modernisation effort comes as part of a broader push to address historical underinvestment in defence, ensuring the New Zealand Defence Force can operate effectively, including alongside its international partners, to deter potential threats and safeguard the nation's security. While the move has been met with mostly positive sentiment, some public debate on the strategic implications of acquiring such technology is also emerging.
