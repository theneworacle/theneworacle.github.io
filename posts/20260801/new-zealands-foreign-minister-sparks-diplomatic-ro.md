A diplomatic row has erupted between New Zealand and China following controversial comments made by New Zealand's Foreign Minister, Winston Peters. During a parliamentary debate regarding New Zealand's COVID-19 response, Peters referred to Chinese-born lawmaker Lawrence Xu-Nan as a 'loudmouth' and controversially instructed him to 'go back to your own country.'

These remarks swiftly drew international attention and strong condemnation. Wang Xiaolong, the Chinese ambassador to New Zealand, publicly denounced the statements via social media, suggesting that such rhetoric reflected more on the speaker than on the subject. Following this, China's embassy in New Zealand lodged a formal diplomatic protest with New Zealand's foreign affairs ministry, expressing strong disapproval of the 'negative China-related remarks.'

Domestically, Peters' comments also faced significant backlash. New Zealand Prime Minister Christopher Luxon and other lawmakers openly criticized the Foreign Minister's statements. However, Peters has remained steadfast in his defense, asserting that his comments were made in the spirit of defending New Zealand's democracy.

The timing of this diplomatic spat is particularly significant given the robust economic relationship between the two nations. China stands as New Zealand's largest trading partner, and such high-profile controversies have the potential to strain these crucial economic ties and broader bilateral relations.
